## Reward engineering: shaping without breaking optimality

A poorly-designed reward (e.g. sparse, only given at task completion) can make RL painfully slow: the agent gets no signal at all until it stumbles onto success by chance. The natural fix, **reward shaping** (adding extra terms to guide learning), has an obvious danger: an arbitrary added reward can change *what the optimal policy actually is*, silently teaching the wrong behavior.

**Potential-based reward shaping** (Ng, Harada & Russell, 1999) avoids this: define a potential function $\Phi(s)$ over states, and shape the reward as

$$R'(s,a,s') := R(s,a,s') + \gamma\Phi(s') - \Phi(s)$$

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-03-ex1-reward-shaping-derivation" />

**This provably preserves the optimal policy**: solving RL-02's Bellman equation with $R'$ gives $Q'(s,a)=Q(s,a)-\Phi(s)$, exactly the original $Q$ shifted by a *state-only* function. Since $\Phi(s)$ doesn't depend on $a$, $\operatorname*{argmax}_aQ'(s,a)=\operatorname*{argmax}_aQ(s,a)$: identical optimal policy, but (with a well-chosen $\Phi$, e.g. an estimate of distance-to-goal) a dramatically denser, more immediately informative reward signal along the way.

## Data collection: the on-policy bottleneck

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-03-ex2-onpolicy-conceptual" />

Many state-of-the-art deep-RL methods (SAC, DDPG, TD3, PPO) require data collected specifically under (something close to) the *current* policy $\pi_\theta$, so that RL-04's Bellman-residual and policy-gradient expectations can be approximated directly by the collected data (formally: SAC is still called "off-policy" because it takes value-function expectations w.r.t. actions freshly sampled from $\pi_\theta$, not literally the old data-collecting policy, but it still needs *reasonably current* rollouts, not arbitrarily stale ones). This is a genuine practical bottleneck on real hardware: unlike classical (discrete, tabular) RL's exploration toolbox (RL-05's optimistic initialization, R-max), a policy generating training rollouts on a physical robot is, by default, an *uninformed*, non-exploring, roughly i.i.d.-per-step noise process (Brownian-motion-like), not a deliberately structured explorer.

**The fix**: replace plain (Brownian-like, uncorrelated) action noise with **structured, temporally-correlated exploration noise**, e.g. pink noise or an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (correlated across nearby time steps, producing coherent excursions rather than jittery, self-canceling ones), or **parameter-space noise** (perturb the policy's own parameters $\theta$ once per episode, rather than perturbing each action independently), both giving qualitatively better state-space coverage per unit of real-robot time than naive per-step random noise, directly RLEARN-01's "good data" principle (frequency/state coverage) applied to online RL exploration specifically.

## Genuine RL applications in robotics

Two concrete, real-world-caliber results worth knowing by name (both trained primarily or entirely in simulation, RLEARN-04's territory): **Gran Turismo Sophy** (Wurman et al., *Nature*, 2022) out-raced champion human drivers in a commercial racing game using deep RL; **champion-level drone racing** (Kaufmann et al., *Nature*, 2023) used deep RL to beat human champion drone racers. Both are genuine demonstrations that the RL-01 through RL-07 + RLEARN-04 toolchain, applied carefully with the reward and data-collection considerations above, reaches genuinely superhuman performance in physically-grounded (if simulation-heavy) domains, not just abstract games.

## Summary

- **Potential-based reward shaping**, $R'=R+\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$, provably preserves the optimal policy (it shifts $Q$ by a state-only function, leaving $\operatorname*{argmax}_a$ unchanged), letting denser rewards be engineered safely.
- Many deep-RL methods need **(near-)on-policy data**, a genuine bottleneck for physical robots; **structured exploration noise** (pink noise, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, parameter-space noise) gives better state coverage per rollout than naive per-step noise, RLEARN-01's "good data" principle applied to online exploration.
- **Gran Turismo Sophy** and **champion-level drone racing** are genuine, superhuman-caliber RL-in-robotics results, both trained primarily in simulation.
