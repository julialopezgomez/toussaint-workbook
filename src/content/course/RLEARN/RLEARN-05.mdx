## Why this matters

RLEARN-02's imitation learning extracts a *policy* from demonstrations. **Inverse RL (IRL)** extracts the *reward function* instead, a genuine instance of imitation learning (same input: demonstrations, no external reward), but a different, arguably deeper target: a reward generalizes to states the demonstrator never visited and to entirely different domains, and a learned agent can in principle *exceed* the demonstrator once it has genuine understanding of what the demonstrator was actually optimizing for.

## General approach

Given expert data (assumed approximately optimal for some unknown $R$), find $R$ such that $J(\pi^*)\geq J(\pi)$ for every other policy $\pi$. Assuming $R(s,a)=w^\top\phi(s,a)$ (linear in given features $\phi$, RLEARN-01/ML-02's familiar "linear in features" trick):

$$J(\pi) = w^\top\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_\pi\Big\{\sum_t\gamma^t\phi(s_t,a_t)\Big\}}_{=:\mu(\pi)}$$

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-05-ex1-linear-reward-derivation" />

so the entire problem reduces to finding $w$ with $\forall\pi\neq\pi^*:w^\top\mu(\pi^*)\geq w^\top\mu(\pi)$.

## Max Margin IRL (Apprenticeship Learning)

Abbeel & Ng (2004): since $\pi^*$ itself isn't literally given, first *estimate* $\mu(\pi^*)$ directly from the demonstration data (the only information about $\pi^*$ actually used). Then iteratively:

```
Apprenticeship-Learning(estimated mu_expert):
  1: initialize pi_0
  2: for i = 0, 1, 2, .. :
  3:     w, t <- argmax_{w,t in R} t   s.t. ||w|| <= 1,
                for all j in {0,..,i}: w^T mu_expert >= w^T mu(pi_j) + t
  4:     pi_{i+1} <- argmax_pi J(pi)     // an ordinary RL subproblem!
```
*(Abbeel & Ng, 2004; as presented in `Lecture-RobotLearning.pdf`, p.75, §1.10:7.)* Step 3 is a genuine max-margin classification problem (OPT-03's territory): find the reward weights $w$ that discriminate the expert's features $\mu(\pi^*)$ from every previously-generated policy $\pi_j$'s features, by as large a margin $t$ as possible. Step 4 uses that reward to compute a *new* candidate policy $\pi_{i+1}$ (an ordinary RL problem, RL-02/RL-04's machinery), which then joins the set to discriminate against next round.

## Max Entropy IRL

Ziebart, Maas, Bagnell & Dey (2008): a different way to handle the demonstrator possibly being noisy/suboptimal. Assume trajectories are generated with probability proportional to their exponentiated return, $P(\xi;w)=\exp\{w^\top\mu(\xi)\}/\int\exp\{w^\top\mu(\xi')\}\,d\xi'$ (a Boltzmann-style distribution, PROB-04's energy-based framing), then find $w$ maximizing the **entropy** of $P(\cdot;w)$ *subject to* matching the expert's observed feature expectations:

$$\min_w\ \int P(\xi;w)\log P(\xi;w)\,d\xi \qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad \mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim P(\xi;w)}\{\mu(\xi)\}=\mu(\pi^*)$$

Intuitively: among *all* reward-induced distributions consistent with the observed data, pick the one making the *fewest* additional assumptions beyond that data, exactly the maximum-entropy principle's standard justification.

## Adversarial IRL

Recall GAN's minimax objective (RLEARN-02): $\min_G\max_D\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_\text{data}}\{\log D(x)\}+\mathbb{E}_{y=G(z)}\{\log[1-D(y)]\}$, training a discriminator to separate real data from generated samples. Max Margin IRL is structurally similar (discriminate the expert from generated policies), suggesting a direct adversarial formulation. **AIRL** (Fu, Luo & Levine, 2018) parameterizes the discriminator on state-action-state triplets as

$$D_{\theta,\phi}(s,a,s') = \frac{\exp\{f_{\theta,\phi}(s,a,s')\}}{\exp\{f_{\theta,\phi}(s,a,s')\}+\pi(a\mid s)}, \qquad f_{\theta,\phi}(s,a,s') := g_\theta(s,a)+\gamma h_\phi(s')-h_\phi(s)$$

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-05-ex3-airl-reward-shaping-connection" />

**This decomposition is exactly RLEARN-03's potential-based reward shaping formula**, $g_\theta(s,a)+\gamma h_\phi(s')-h_\phi(s)$ has the identical algebraic shape as $R(s,a)+\gamma\Phi(s')-\Phi(s)$, with $h_\phi$ playing $\Phi$'s role. RLEARN-03 showed this exact form leaves the optimal policy unchanged while reshaping $Q$ by $-\Phi(s)$; here, training $f_{\theta,\phi}$ this way means $f_{\theta,\phi}(s,a,s')\approx r(s,a)+\gamma V(s')-V(s)=A(s,a)$, the **advantage function**. The training automatically gives $g_\theta$ "reward semantics" and $h_\phi$ "value semantics," a genuinely elegant emergent structure, purely from choosing this particular functional form for the discriminator.

## Preference-based RL

A different route around "demonstrations may be hard to elicit or noisy": instead of full demonstrations, collect **preferences**, pairwise comparisons $\xi_i\succ\xi_j$ ("this behavior is better than that one," easy for a human to judge even without being able to demonstrate optimal behavior directly). Find $R$ such that $\xi_i\succ\xi_j\Rightarrow\sum_tR(s_t^i,a_t^i)>\sum_tR(s_t^j,a_t^j)$. Assuming noisy human judgments (the **Bradley-Terry model**):

$$P(\xi_i\succ\xi_j;R) = \frac{\exp\{\sum_tR(s_t^i,a_t^i)\}}{\exp\{\sum_tR(s_t^i,a_t^i)\}+\exp\{\sum_tR(s_t^j,a_t^j)\}}$$

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-05-ex4-bradley-terry-numeric" />

trained by maximizing $\sum_{\xi_i\succ\xi_j}\log P(\xi_i\succ\xi_j;R_\psi)$ over all human-provided preferences (Christiano et al., 2017, "Deep RL from Human Preferences"). This is essentially a *sequential* generalization of PROB's/ML's ranking-loss idea, and it directly avoids IRL's usual bottleneck (needing many, often hard-to-elicit, full demonstrations).

## Summary

- General IRL: assuming $R$ linear in features $\phi$, $J(\pi)=w^\top\mu(\pi)$; find $w$ discriminating the expert's $\mu(\pi^*)$ from other policies.
- **Max Margin IRL** alternates a max-margin discrimination step (find $w$) with an RL step (find a new policy to discriminate against).
- **Max Entropy IRL** picks the maximum-entropy reward-induced distribution consistent with the expert's observed feature expectations, robust to demonstrator noise.
- **Adversarial IRL**'s discriminator decomposition $g_\theta(s,a)+\gamma h_\phi(s')-h_\phi(s)$ is exactly RLEARN-03's reward-shaping form, automatically recovering a reward ($g_\theta$) and value function ($h_\phi$), with the trained quantity converging to the advantage function.
- **Preference-based RL** replaces full demonstrations with pairwise preferences, using the Bradley-Terry model to fit a reward directly from noisy comparative judgments.
- Value Alignment (the broader "how do we even define a good objective" question) is already covered in RL-07 and not repeated here.
