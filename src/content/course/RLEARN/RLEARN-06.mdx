## Grasp labels are privileged-teacher, twice over

**Model-based labels** (GraspNet-1Billion, DexNet 2.0): for every point in a scene, every approach direction, offset, roll, and width, compute MANIP-01's **force-closure metric** directly, requiring exact, ground-truth object poses and shapes. **Force-closure theory is the origin of the "intelligence" here**; the learned network's actual job is only to transfer that theory to work from real, noisy RGB-D input, where exact poses/shapes aren't available.

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-06-ex1-privileged-teacher-grasp-labels" />

**Simulation-based labels** (ACRONYM, Eppner, Mousavian & Fox, 2021): throw random objects (from a shape dataset) into a rigid-body physics simulator, generate candidate grasps, close and lift the gripper, and directly *measure* success (in-hand motion during lift) rather than computing a force-closure score at all.

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-06-ex2-simulation-labels-numeric" />

Both are the *same* underlying pattern as RLEARN-02's privileged teacher: some source of ground truth unavailable at deployment (exact shapes/poses for force closure; a full physics simulator for ACRONYM) generates labels, and the learned network only has to reproduce that source's judgments from realistic sensor input. **The physics simulator (Newton-Euler + contact models, DYN-01) is, in this precise sense, "the origin of intelligence" for ACRONYM-style data**, exactly as force-closure theory is for GraspNet/DexNet.

## What grasp-prediction architectures actually predict

**GraspNet-1Billion** (Fang et al., 2020): given a point cloud, first classify each point $p$ for viability/success, then predict the remaining grasp parameters (direction $D$, rotation $R$, width $w$), with separate loss functions per part, a genuinely structured, staged prediction rather than one monolithic regression. **AnyGrasp** (GraspNet 2, Fang et al., 2023) extends this to temporally-stable, dynamic predictions (grasping a moving object consistently across frames, not just a single static snapshot). Other notable lines: antipodal-grasp detection directly in point clouds (Ten Pas et al.), Contact-GraspNet (efficient 6-DOF grasps in cluttered scenes), and diffusion-based grasp/motion cost functions (Urain et al., 2023, directly RLEARN-02's diffusion-model machinery applied to grasp generation).

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-06-ex3-architecture-conceptual" />

**The real bottleneck, per the source's own stated view**: given good data, architecture design is comparatively solved; **data generation is the actual key**, and existing methods still have real deficits, especially for more complex hands than the standard parallel-jaw gripper.

## Manipulation beyond pick-and-place

Grasping is only one instance of manipulation. **Articulated objects** (doors, drawers, scissors) need a different representation entirely: **FlowBot3D** (Eisner, Zhang & Held, 2024) learns a mapping from every point $p$ on an object's surface to a **flow field** $F_p\in\mathbb{R}^3$ (the instantaneous direction that point would move under successful manipulation), assuming the gripper can attach anywhere on the surface, a fundamentally different target than "predict one good grasp pose."

<ExerciseCard id="RLEARN-06-ex4-beyond-mdp-conceptual" />

**Manipulation learning is often described as beyond the MDP/RL framework** precisely because of this: rather than learning a low-level state $\mapsto$ action policy (RL-01's MDP setting), most of the genuinely successful manipulation-learning work instead predicts *structured intermediate targets*, grasps in an RGB-D scene, manipulability/flow fields for articulated objects, keypoints/waypoints of interaction, which are then executed using ordinary model-based control (DYN-03's operational-space control, or KIN-03's IK) rather than a learned policy at all. This is worth internalizing as a genuine, still-open research question, not settled: general-purpose manipulation (pushing, throwing, rope/cloth manipulation, tool use) remains, in the source's own assessment, "far away from truly understanding" as a unified learning problem.

## Summary

- Both **model-based** (force-closure metric on known shapes) and **simulation-based** (physics simulator ground truth) grasp-label generation are instances of RLEARN-02's privileged-teacher pattern: some ground-truth source generates labels, the network transfers those judgments to realistic sensor input.
- **GraspNet/DexNet-style architectures** predict grasp success/viability first, then geometric parameters, in structured stages, not one monolithic regression; data generation, not architecture, is the field's real bottleneck.
- **Manipulation learning beyond pick-and-place** (articulated objects, flow fields, general manipulation) is often "beyond the MDP/RL framework": most successful work predicts structured intermediate targets (grasps, flow fields, keypoints), executed by classical model-based control, rather than learning a low-level policy directly.
