## Three threads that run through all of it

**Local vs. global methods.** Newton's method (OPT-01) and Laplace approximation (PROB-06) both commit to a single local quadratic model; PRM/RRT (PLAN-02) commit to whatever narrow-passage coverage they happen to sample. Every one of these trades completeness for tractability, and every one of them can silently fail exactly where the local approximation breaks down (far from the expansion point, near an obstacle-adjacent narrow passage). This isn't three separate limitations, it's one recurring tradeoff showing up in optimization, probability, and planning alike.

**The known-model-to-model-free spectrum.** LQR (DYN-04) assumes the full dynamics model and cost are known and solves in closed form; RL-02's value iteration assumes the model is known but doesn't require it to be linear/quadratic; RL-03's Q-learning and RLEARN-01's dynamics learning assume nothing is known and estimate everything from data. Moving along this spectrum trades exactness and sample efficiency for applicability to systems you can't write a model for.

<ExerciseCard id="REV1-01-ex2-spectrum-application" />

**Metric-awareness.** MATH-03B/MATH-05 establish that $\nabla f(x)$ is a covector, not a vector, and that steepest descent under a non-Euclidean metric $G$ needs the covariant gradient $-G^{-1}\nabla f(x)$. This isn't just an abstract linear-algebra point: it's exactly why potential-field control (PLAN-01) can behave badly for a robot with mixed units, and it's the same mathematical object (a positive-definite matrix certifying a quadratic form) that reappears as DYN-06's Lyapunov function and DYN-04's LQR value function $P$.
