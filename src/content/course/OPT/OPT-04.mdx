## Why this matters

Convexity is the property that separates "optimization problems you can actually solve reliably" from "optimization problems where you're at the mercy of your starting point." Recognizing when a subproblem is convex (or can be made convex, e.g. via a QP approximation) is a constant judgment call in trajectory optimization and motion planning, exactly the kind of problem your own manipulation-planning research deals with.

## Convexity: why it matters so much

<ExerciseCard id="OPT-04-ex1-why-convexity-matters" />

This is the single biggest reason convex problems (including LPs and QPs, below) are so valuable: any local optimization method (gradient descent, Newton) that finds *a* local minimum has automatically found *the* global minimum, no need to worry about getting stuck.

## Linear and quadratic programs

A **linear program (LP)** has a linear objective and linear inequality constraints: $\min_x c^\top x$ s.t. $Ax\le b$. A **quadratic program (QP)** allows a quadratic (convex, if the quadratic term's matrix is positive semi-definite) objective with the same linear constraints. Both are convex whenever correctly set up, so local solutions are global.

## The log-barrier method

One practical way to solve a constrained convex problem: replace each inequality constraint with a penalty term that blows up near the boundary, turning the constrained problem into a sequence of unconstrained ones.

<ExerciseCard id="OPT-04-ex2-log-barrier-derivation" />

**Worked example**: for $g(x)=x-5$ (constraint $x\le5$) with barrier weight $t=1$, the barrier gradient contribution $-\dfrac{1}{t\,g(x)}$ evaluates to $1$ at $x=4$ but $10$ at $x=4.9$, a tenfold increase for moving just $0.9$ closer to the boundary. *(Verified symbolically with SymPy while authoring this module.)* This is exactly the "increasingly strong repulsion" behavior from the derivation above, made concrete.

In practice, the log-barrier method solves a *sequence* of unconstrained problems with $t$ gradually increased (weakening the barrier), each solution used to warm-start the next, converging toward the true constrained solution as $t\to\infty$.

## SQP, briefly

**Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP)** handles general (not necessarily convex) constrained problems by repeatedly forming a local QP approximation (linearize the constraints, use a quadratic model of the Lagrangian, exactly the Newton/Gauss-Newton idea from OPT-01/OPT-02 applied to the constrained KKT system) and solving that QP exactly, then repeating at the new point. It's covered here at a conceptual level; a full derivation is a natural extension once you need it for a specific problem.

## Summary

- A local minimum of a convex function is automatically global, this is what makes convex problems tractable.
- LPs and QPs are convex when correctly formulated (linear or convex-quadratic objective, linear constraints).
- The log-barrier method converts a constrained problem into a sequence of unconstrained ones, using a penalty that blows up near the constraint boundary, preventing infeasibility by construction.
- SQP extends the Newton/Gauss-Newton idea to general constrained problems via repeated local QP approximations.
