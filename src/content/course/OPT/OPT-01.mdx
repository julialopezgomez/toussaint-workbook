## Why this matters

This module is where the Math block's tools (gradients, Hessians, Taylor expansion, the covariant gradient) start doing real work: every optimizer used later in this course, from trajectory optimization to training a neural network, is some variation on "take a step informed by the local gradient and curvature." Newton's method, derived properly here, is also the direct ancestor of Gauss-Newton (next module) and the optimal-control formulation your own manipulation-planning research builds on.

## Gradient descent and the step-size problem

The simplest optimizer, gradient descent, updates $x \leftarrow x - \alpha \nabla f(x)$. It's cheap (only needs the gradient) but its performance depends heavily on choosing $\alpha$ well.

<ExerciseCard id="OPT-01-ex1-why-stepsize-matters" />

## Newton's method: use curvature, not just slope

Rather than guessing a step size, use the second-order Taylor model itself to choose the *best* step, assuming (optimistically) that the quadratic model is exact.

<ExerciseCard id="OPT-01-ex2-newton-derivation" />

**Stop and try**: check that Newton's step reduces to something sensible in 1D: for $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, $\delta^* = -f'(x)/f''(x)$. Confirm this matches $-H(x)^{-1}\nabla f(x)$ in the $n=1$ case.

## Worked example

Let $f(x) = \tfrac12 x^\top A x - b^\top x$ with $A=\begin{pmatrix}4&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$, $b=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-2\end{pmatrix}$, starting at $x_0=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix}$. Since $\nabla f(x)=Ax-b$ and $H=A$ (constant, since $f$ is quadratic):

$$\nabla f(x_0) = A(0)-b = \begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \delta^* = -A^{-1}\nabla f(x_0)$$

Solving $A\delta^*=-\nabla f(x_0)$ (rather than explicitly inverting $A$) gives $\delta^* \approx \begin{pmatrix}0.4545\\-0.8182\end{pmatrix}$, so $x_1 = x_0+\delta^* \approx \begin{pmatrix}0.4545\\-0.8182\end{pmatrix}$.

*(Verified with NumPy: $x_1$ matches $A^{-1}b$, the exact minimizer, exactly, since a single Newton step on a genuinely quadratic $f$ always lands exactly on the minimizer, not just approximately closer to it.)*

## When the quadratic model isn't exact: line search

For a non-quadratic $f$, the quadratic model is only locally accurate, so a full Newton step $\delta^*$ might overshoot. **Backtracking line search** addresses this by trying $\alpha=1$ first, then shrinking $\alpha$ (e.g. by half each time) until the step satisfies the Armijo condition.

## Summary

- Fixed-step gradient descent is fragile: too large risks divergence, too small wastes iterations.
- Newton's method, $\delta^* = -H(x)^{-1}\nabla f(x)$, is the exact minimizer of the local second-order Taylor model, derived by differentiating that model and setting it to zero, the same "set the derivative to zero" idea from ordinary calculus, applied to a model rather than $f$ itself.
- Backtracking line search with the Armijo condition guarantees a meaningful fraction of the predicted decrease is actually achieved, protecting against both overshoot and negligible-progress steps.
