## Why this matters

Almost every real robotics optimization problem has constraints: joint limits, collision avoidance, dynamics feasibility, contact conditions. The Lagrangian and KKT conditions are the foundation every constrained solver (interior point, SQP, augmented Lagrangian, all covered later) builds on. Getting comfortable solving small constrained problems by hand here makes those later methods feel like variations on one idea, rather than a new toolbox each time.

## The Lagrangian

The Lagrangian packages the objective and constraint into a single function whose *unconstrained* stationarity condition captures the correct geometric picture:

$$L(x,\kappa) = f(x) + \kappa\, h(x)$$

<ExerciseCard id="OPT-03-ex2-lagrangian-derivation" />

**Stop and try**: before continuing, note what makes this trick work: $\nabla_\kappa L(x,\kappa) = h(x)$, so setting *that* to zero recovers the constraint itself. The Lagrangian's full stationarity conditions ($\nabla_x L=0$ and $\nabla_\kappa L=0$ together) exactly recover both the parallel-gradients condition and the original constraint, as a single unconstrained problem in $(x,\kappa)$.

## Worked example

Minimize $f(x)=\tfrac12(x_1^2+x_2^2)$ subject to $x_1-x_2=1$. Lagrangian: $L(x,\kappa) = \tfrac12(x_1^2+x_2^2)+\kappa(x_1-x_2-1)$.

$$\nabla_x L = \begin{pmatrix}x_1+\kappa\\x_2-\kappa\end{pmatrix}=0 \implies x_1=-\kappa,\ x_2=\kappa$$

Substituting into the constraint: $-\kappa-\kappa=1 \implies \kappa=-0.5$, giving $x_1^*=0.5$, $x_2^*=-0.5$.

*(Check: $0.5-(-0.5)=1$ ✓. Verified with a direct linear solve of the full KKT system while authoring this module.)*

## Inequality constraints and complementary slackness

For $g(x)\le 0$, the full KKT conditions add a subtlety: the constraint might or might not be "touching" its boundary at the optimum. **Complementary slackness**, $\lambda\, g(x^*)=0$, handles both cases in one equation.

<ExerciseCard id="OPT-03-ex4-complementary-slackness-challenge" />

## Summary

- The constrained stationarity condition is that $\nabla f(x^*)$ is parallel to $\nabla h(x^*)$ (perpendicular to the constraint surface), not zero.
- The Lagrangian $L=f+\kappa h$ packages this into an ordinary unconstrained stationarity condition, $\nabla_x L=0$ recovers the parallel-gradients condition, $\nabla_\kappa L=0$ recovers the constraint itself.
- For inequality constraints, complementary slackness ($\lambda g(x^*)=0$) enforces: inactive constraints have zero multiplier, active constraints (positive multiplier) must be exactly tight.

## Retrieval questions

1. Derive the KKT stationarity condition from the Lagrangian, without looking back.
2. Why does $\nabla_\kappa L(x,\kappa)=0$ recover the original constraint?
3. If a Lagrange multiplier for an inequality constraint comes out negative in a proposed solution, what does that tell you (hint: multipliers for $g(x)\le0$ must satisfy $\lambda\ge0$)?
