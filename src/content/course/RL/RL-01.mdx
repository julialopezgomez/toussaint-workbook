## Why this matters

DYN-04 already derived the Bellman equation once, for deterministic dynamics and a cost to minimize. This module derives the *same* recursive idea for the genuinely more general setting the rest of this block lives in: stochastic transitions, rewards to maximize, and (until RL-02) no assumption that you even know the underlying model. Nearly everything in RL, value iteration, TD-learning, Q-learning, policy gradients, MCTS, is a different way of exploiting this one equation.

## Markov Decision Processes

A **Markov Decision Process (MDP)** is defined by an initial state distribution $P(s_0)$, transition probabilities $P(s'\mid s,a)$, and an expected reward $R(s,a)=\mathbb{E}\{r\mid s,a\}$ (assumed **stationary**: independent of absolute time $t$). The **Markov property**: $s_t$ is a valid state description iff the future ($s_{t'>t}$) is conditionally independent of the past ($s_{t'<t}$) given $s_t$, i.e. $s_t$ captures *everything* about the world relevant to predicting what happens next; there are no additional hidden variables the process depends on beyond $s_t$.

<ExerciseCard id="RL-01-ex1-markov-property-conceptual" />

The goal is a **policy** ($a=\pi(s)$ deterministic, or $\pi(a\mid s)=P(a\mid s;\pi)$ stochastic) maximizing the **expected return**:

$$J^\pi = \mathbb{E}\Big\{\sum_t\gamma^tr_t\ \Big|\ \pi\Big\} = \mathbb{E}\{r_0+\gamma r_1+\gamma^2r_2+\cdots\mid\pi\}, \qquad \gamma\in[0,1)$$

with $s_0\sim P(s_0)$. This is DYN-04's optimal-control objective, translated: $c(x,u)\to-r$ (minimizing cost becomes maximizing reward, just a sign flip), $x_{t+1}=f(x_t,u_t)\to s_{t+1}\sim P(s'\mid s_t,a_t)$ (deterministic dynamics become a distribution over next states), and a new ingredient, the **discount factor** $\gamma$ (weighting near-term reward more than distant reward; DYN-04's finite-horizon sum needs no such factor, but an infinite-horizon MDP sum would otherwise not converge for a policy earning reward forever).

## Value functions and Q-functions

The **value function** of policy $\pi$ is the expected return starting from state $s$:

$$V^\pi(s) = \mathbb{E}\Big\{\sum_t\gamma^tr_t\ \Big|\ s_0=s;\pi\Big\}$$

exactly DYN-04's $V_t(x)$ (optimal cost-to-go), now for a *fixed* policy $\pi$ (not necessarily optimal) and *expected* (not deterministic) return. The **Q-function** additionally fixes the first action:

$$Q^\pi(s,a) = \mathbb{E}\Big\{\sum_t\gamma^tr_t\ \Big|\ s_0=s,a_0=a;\pi\Big\}$$

The **optimal value function** is $V^*(s)=\max_\pi V^\pi(s)$; a policy $\pi^*$ is **optimal** iff $V^{\pi^*}(s)=V^*(s)$ for *every* state $s$ simultaneously (a genuinely strong requirement: in MDPs, at least one deterministic optimal policy always exists satisfying it).

## The recursive property of the value function

<ExerciseCard id="RL-01-ex2-recursive-value-derivation" />

Splitting off the first reward and conditioning on the resulting state:

$$V^\pi(s) = R(s,\pi(s)) + \gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,\pi(s))\,V^\pi(s')$$

exactly DYN-04's Bellman-equation derivation (split the sum, apply Bellman's principle to the remainder), now with an **expectation over stochastic next states** replacing DYN-04's single deterministic successor.

## The Bellman optimality equation

> **Theorem (Bellman optimality).** The optimal value function satisfies
> $$V^*(s) = \max_a\Big[R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\,V^*(s')\Big]$$
> and $\pi^*(s)=\operatorname*{argmax}_a[\,\cdots\,]$ is an optimal policy.

*(Sketch of proof: if $\pi^*$ selected any action other than the maximizer, the policy $\pi'$ agreeing with $\pi^*$ everywhere except $\pi'(s):=\operatorname*{argmax}_a[\cdots]$ would achieve strictly higher value at $s$, contradicting $\pi^*$'s optimality.)* This is exactly DYN-04's Bellman equation, $\min\to\max$ (cost to reward) and a deterministic successor replaced by an expectation, the *same* dynamic-programming intuition: if you already know how to act optimally from every possible next state $s'$ (i.e. you know $V^*(s')$), deciding optimally *now* is a single one-step lookahead.

## Summary

- An **MDP** is $(P(s_0),P(s'\mid s,a),R(s,a))$, stationary; the **Markov property** says $s_t$ captures everything relevant to the future, with no hidden extra dependence on the past.
- The objective is the **expected discounted return** $J^\pi=\mathbb{E}\{\sum_t\gamma^tr_t\mid\pi\}$; $V^\pi(s)$ and $Q^\pi(s,a)$ are its state- and state-action-conditioned versions.
- $V^\pi$ satisfies a **recursive (Bellman) equation**, derived exactly like DYN-04's, generalized to stochastic transitions.
- The **Bellman optimality equation** for $V^*$ generalizes DYN-04's deterministic Bellman equation: $\min\to\max$ (cost to reward), deterministic successor to expectation over $P(s'\mid s,a)$.
