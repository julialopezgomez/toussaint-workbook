## Why this matters

RLEARN is the single densest block in this workbook, 9 modules covering dynamics learning through TAMP. Unlike REV1 (which had to compress 11 blocks), RLEARN already did a lot of its own internal synthesis: RLEARN-00 opened with a taxonomy, and later modules constantly cross-referenced earlier ones. This review is shorter for exactly that reason: it's a final consolidation pass, not a rescue mission for content that never got tied together.

## RLEARN's own throughline

RLEARN-00 framed the entire block as **shortcuts to specific pieces of the model-based control stack** (DYN-01 through DYN-04, KIN-03) that break down when you can't write an accurate model, can't solve constraints in closed form, or don't have direct state access. Every module since is, in that framing, one row of RLEARN-00's own table: dynamics learning replaces system identification, policy learning replaces optimal control, grasp/plan prediction replaces constraint-solving and state estimation.

## One pattern, four modules

The **privileged-teacher pattern** (train with information unavailable at deployment, generate labels, then train a deployment-time model on only what's actually available) shows up explicitly in at least four places: RLEARN-02's DAgger (a privileged/queryable expert), RLEARN-04's sim2real (a privileged simulator with exact dynamics parameters), RLEARN-06's grasp labels (a privileged force-closure computation or physics simulator), and RLEARN-07's Deep Visual Constraints (privileged simulation trial-and-error data). It's not four separate tricks, it's one idea, reused because it's genuinely the right tool whenever ground truth is available at training time but not at deployment.

## Where CAP-01 is headed

CAP-01 is explicitly framed as connecting the whole curriculum to your own implemented research, not new taught content.
