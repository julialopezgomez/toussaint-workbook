## Pose and $SO(3)$

> **Definition.** A **pose** is a translation $p\in\mathbb{R}^3$ and a rotation $R\in SO(3)$. $R$ is **orthonormal** ($R^\top R=I$, $R^{-1}=R^\top$, columns/rows are orthogonal unit vectors) with $\det(R)=1$ (ruling out reflections, $\det=-1$).

For world frame $(o,e_{1:3})$ and body frame $(o',e'_{1:3})$ with $e'_i=\sum_jR_{ji}e_j$ (the new basis vectors are $R$'s *columns*, in the old basis — exactly MATH-03's coordinate-transformation machinery), a point with body-frame coordinates $x'$ has world coordinates $x = p + Rx'$.

## Alternative rotation representations

**Euler angles** (e.g. yaw-pitch-roll, 3-2-1) describe a rotation as three consecutive rotations about (possibly moving) axes. Compact, but suffer a severe flaw:

<SourceFigure
  src="/figures/lecture-robotics/fig2-gimbal-lock.png"
  alt="Diagram of a gimbal mechanism showing yaw, pitch, and roll rings; when two rotation axes align, one degree of freedom is lost"
  caption="Gimbal lock: if two of the three rotation axes align, one degree of freedom of rotation is blocked entirely, a genuine 'singularity' of the Euler-angle parameterization."
  source="Lecture-Robotics.pdf"
  page="15"
/>

**Gimbal lock**: if two of the three rotation axes align (e.g. a 3-1-3 sequence with the second rotation at $0$ or $\pi$), one degree of freedom of rotation is blocked entirely, a genuine singularity of the parameterization, not just a numerical inconvenience.

The **rotation vector** $w\in\mathbb{R}^3$ avoids this: $|w|=\theta$ is the rotation angle, $\hat w=w/\theta$ the axis. Applying it to a vector $v$ is **Rodrigues' formula**:

$$w\cdot v = \cos\theta\,v + \sin\theta\,(\hat w\times v) + (1-\cos\theta)\,\hat w(\hat w^\top v)$$

Defining the **skew matrix** $\text{skew}(w)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-w_3&w_2\\w_3&0&-w_1\\-w_2&w_1&0\end{pmatrix}$ (so $\text{skew}(w)v = w\times v$, turning the cross product into ordinary matrix multiplication), the equivalent matrix form is the **exponential map**:

$$R(w) = \exp(\text{skew}(w)) = \cos\theta\,I + \sin\theta\,\widehat{\text{skew}}(w) + (1-\cos\theta)\,\hat w\hat w^\top, \qquad \widehat{\text{skew}}(w):=\text{skew}(\hat w)$$

## Angular velocity

For a body rotating with angular velocity $w\in\mathbb{R}^3$ (axis $\hat w$, speed $|w|$ rad/s), and orientation $R(t)$:

$$\dot R(t) = \text{skew}(w)\,R(t)$$

<ExerciseCard id="KIN-01-ex3-rdot-derivation" />

This is worth having as a reflex: $\dot R$ must always have this form, since $R(t)$ staying in $SO(3)$ for all $t$ (orthonormal, $\det=1$) is a genuine constraint on what its derivative can look like, exactly analogous to how a point moving on a sphere must have a velocity tangent to the sphere. Given $R(0)=I$, the solution is $R(t)=\exp(t\,\text{skew}(w))$, another instance of the exponential map, this time generating a whole trajectory rather than a single fixed rotation.

## Homogeneous transformations

Packaging $p$ and $R$ into one $4\times4$ matrix makes chaining poses trivial matrix multiplication:

$$T = \begin{pmatrix}R&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix} \in\mathbb{R}^{4\times4}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix}x^A\\1\end{pmatrix} = T_{A\to B}\begin{pmatrix}x^B\\1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}Rx^B+t\\1\end{pmatrix}$$

**A genuinely easy point to get backwards**: $T_{A\to B}$ describes the *forward* **frame** transform (frame $B$'s pose relative to $A$), but the *backward* **coordinate** transform ($x^B\mapsto x^A$, not $x^A\mapsto x^B$). This is exactly MATH-03's vector/coordinate contra-variance distinction, made concrete: frames (like basis vectors) transform covariantly, coordinates transform contra-variantly. Composition chains left to right along frames, matching right to left along the coordinate transforms actually applied: $T_{W\to C}=T_{W\to A}T_{A\to B}T_{B\to C}$, and $x^W = T_{W\to A}T_{A\to B}T_{B\to C}\,x^C$.

## Summary

- Pose = translation + $R\in SO(3)$ ($R^\top R=I$, $\det R=1$); $x=p+Rx'$ converts body- to world-frame coordinates.
- Euler angles are compact but suffer gimbal lock (a genuine singularity, not just numerical); the rotation vector + Rodrigues' formula / exponential map $R(w)=\exp(\text{skew}(w))$ avoids it, at the cost of a $\sin/\cos$ evaluation per use.
- $\dot R(t)=\text{skew}(w)R(t)$ is the only form a rotation matrix's derivative can take, a direct consequence of $R(t)\in SO(3)$ for all $t$.
- Homogeneous transforms $T\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times4}$ chain poses via ordinary matrix multiplication; $T_{A\to B}$ is the forward frame transform but the backward coordinate transform, exactly MATH-03's contra/covariance distinction in disguise.
