## Why this matters

Splines are DYN-04's trajectory optimization made concrete and closed-form for the simplest useful case (piecewise-cubic paths), and they're what an actual reference trajectory generator produces before DYN-03's tracking control ever runs. The single-cubic-piece result here is also a genuinely elegant closure: it's the *exact* solution to a minimum-acceleration optimal control problem, connecting straight back to DYN-04's machinery.

## A single cubic spline piece

A cubic polynomial $x(t)=at^3+bt^2+ct+d$ has 4 coefficients, matching exactly 4 boundary conditions: $x(0)=x_0,\dot x(0)=v_0,x(\tau)=x_1,\dot x(\tau)=v_1$ (a spline piece of duration $\tau$).

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-05-ex1-cubic-coefficients-derivation" />

$$d=x_0,\quad c=v_0, \quad a = \frac{1}{\tau^3}\big[2(x_0-x_1)+\tau(v_0+v_1)\big], \quad b = \frac{1}{\tau^2}\big[3(x_1-x_0)-\tau(2v_0+v_1)\big]$$

**This cubic is the exact solution to a minimum-acceleration optimal control problem**: $\min_x\int_0^\tau\ddot x(t)^2\,dt$ subject to the same four boundary conditions, a direct instance of [DYN-04](/course/DYN/DYN-04)'s optimal control formulation with $c(x,u)=\ddot x^2$. The minimal cost has a clean closed form:

$$\int_0^\tau\ddot x(t)^2\,dt = \frac{12}{\tau^3}D^2+\frac1\tau V^2, \qquad D:=(x_1-x_0)-\frac\tau2(v_0+v_1),\ \ V:=v_1-v_0$$

*(Verified symbolically with SymPy while authoring this module: substituting the coefficients above and integrating $\ddot x(t)^2$ reproduces this formula exactly.)* Written as $\tilde D^\top\tilde D+\tilde V^\top\tilde V$ with $\tilde D=\sqrt{12}\,\tau^{-3/2}D$, $\tilde V=\tau^{-1/2}V$, this is a genuine sum-of-squares, exactly the Gauss-Newton-friendly form from OPT-04/DYN-04.

## Timing-optimal control: choosing $\tau$ itself

A plain PD controller ($\ddot x_{\text{des}}=K_p(x_{\text{ref}}-x)+K_d(\dot x_{\text{ref}}-\dot x)$) only converges *exponentially*: it never reaches the target in a finite, predictable time, and its accelerations are disproportionately large far from the target and small near it. An alternative: treat $\tau$ itself as a decision variable, minimizing $\alpha\tau+\int_0^\tau\ddot x(t)^2\,dt$ (trading off "get there sooner" against "don't accelerate too hard") using the closed-form cost above. This gives a **definite expected time-to-target** and naturally moderate accelerations, reactively re-solved for $\tau$ each control cycle (using the *current* state as the new starting boundary condition).

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-05-ex3-timing-conceptual" />

*(A note on precision: the source states a specific closed-form solution for the optimal $\tau$ in the co-linear case. Working through the actual stationarity condition $\partial_\tau[\alpha\tau+\text{cost}(\tau)]=0$ independently while authoring this module, the resulting equation is a genuine quartic in $\tau$ without a simple square-root closed form in general; rather than present an unverified formula, this module states the optimization problem precisely and lets you set up the stationarity condition yourself, exactly the kind of "check by direct verification" habit this whole workbook has tried to build.)*

## Hermite splines: continuity and degrees of freedom

A **Hermite spline** is specified by knots $t_0,..,t_m$ and waypoints $x_i$, velocities $v_i$ at each knot ($m$ cubic pieces total). A genuinely useful piece of bookkeeping: how many free parameters remain once you require a given continuity class?

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-05-ex4-continuity-counting" />

| Continuity | Meaning | Free parameters ($m$ pieces, 1D) |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{C}^0$ | positions continuous | $1+3m$ |
| $\mathcal{C}^1$ | velocities continuous | $2+2m$ |
| $\mathcal{C}^2$ | accelerations continuous | $3+m$ |
| $\mathcal{C}^3$ | jerk continuous | $4$ (independent of $m$!) |

$\mathcal{C}^3$ having only 4 parameters regardless of the number of pieces is exactly why continuous-jerk paths are only achievable with a *single* piece (a degree-3 polynomial, 4 coefficients, has no room left for extra pieces once jerk-continuity is demanded); $\mathcal{C}^2$ (continuous accelerations, the typical choice) is the natural middle ground, offering $3+m$ free parameters (the piece durations $\tau_{1:m}$ and interior velocities) to optimize.

## B-splines: control points instead of explicit waypoints

A **B-spline** instead expresses the path as a linear combination of $K{+}1$ **control points** $z_0,..,z_K$: $x(t)=\sum_{i=0}^KB_{i,p}(t)z_i$, where the blending functions $B_{i,p}(t)$ (themselves called B-splines) sum to 1 at every $t$, so $x(t)$ always lies in the convex hull of the control points. Unlike Hermite splines, the path generally does **not** pass exactly through the control points, but a one-time matrix inversion (the $B_{i,p}$ evaluated at the desired waypoint times form a band-diagonal matrix, invertible in $O(m)$) lets you *choose* control points that make it pass through given waypoints when needed.

The key structural fact: **a degree-$p$ B-spline is always exactly $(p{-}1)$-order differentiable** (its derivative is a linear combination of degree-$(p{-}1)$ B-splines, recursively), matching the $\mathcal{C}^{p-1}$ continuity class directly, e.g. a cubic ($p=3$) B-spline has continuous accelerations, matching $\mathcal{C}^2$ Hermite splines exactly, they're just alternative parameterizations of the same continuity class, convertible back and forth via the same matrix-inversion idea. The gradient of a B-spline path with respect to its control points is trivial (linear), which is exactly why trajectory optimizers (e.g. KOMO-style) often re-parameterize a fine-grained discretized trajectory through a much lower-dimensional set of B-spline control points, solving a smaller NLP with (via the chain rule) gradients that pull back trivially to the original fine-grained representation.

## Summary

- A cubic spline piece's 4 coefficients come directly from 4 boundary conditions; the piece is the exact minimizer of $\int\ddot x^2dt$ subject to those conditions, a direct instance of DYN-04's optimal control, with a closed-form sum-of-squares cost.
- Timing-optimal control treats the piece duration $\tau$ itself as a decision variable, giving a definite time-to-target and moderate accelerations, unlike plain PD's asymptotic-only convergence.
- Hermite splines: the continuity class ($\mathcal{C}^0,..,\mathcal{C}^3$) directly determines how many free parameters remain; $\mathcal{C}^3$ (continuous jerk) only admits a single piece.
- B-splines parameterize a path via control points and blending functions; degree $p$ gives exactly $\mathcal{C}^{p-1}$ continuity, and control-point gradients are trivial, useful for re-parameterizing large trajectory-optimization problems.
