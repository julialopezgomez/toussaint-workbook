## Why this matters

This module is where DYN-01's equations of motion and DYN-02's point-mass control combine: general robot dynamics reduce to $M(q)\ddot q+F(q,\dot q)=u$, and controlling that turns out to always reduce, one way or another, to giving every relevant coordinate DYN-02's 1D point-mass PD behavior. Operational-space control is also a direct, satisfying payoff of KIN-03's IK derivation: identical algebra, applied to accelerations instead of positions.

## General robot dynamics, and two uses

Recapping DYN-01 in the notation this module needs: $M(q)\ddot q+F(q,\dot q)=u$, with $M(q)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ symmetric PD (invertible), $F(q,\dot q)$ collecting Coriolis/centrifugal and gravity terms. Two direct uses, once $M(q)$ and $F(q,\dot q)$ are computable:

$$\textbf{Inverse dynamics: } u = M(q)\ddot q^*+F(q,\dot q) \qquad \textbf{Forward dynamics: } \ddot q^* = M(q)^{-1}(u-F(q,\dot q))$$

Inverse dynamics answers "what torques do I need for this desired acceleration?"; forward dynamics answers "what acceleration results from these torques?" (used to *simulate* the system, e.g. via RK4 from [ODE-03](/course/ODE/ODE-03)).

**Computing $M(q),F(q,\dot q)$ efficiently**: the naive per-body-Jacobian sum from DYN-01 works but isn't the fastest route. The **Newton-Euler recursion** computes inverse dynamics $u=\text{NE}(q,\dot q,\ddot q^*)$ in $O(n)$ by propagating forces down the kinematic tree (forward pass: compute velocities/accelerations per link from the base outward; backward pass: compute joint torques from the leaves inward, via per-body force balance), without ever forming $M(q)$ explicitly. Useful side effect: calling it with different inputs isolates specific terms, $\text{NE}(q,0,0)=G(q)$ (gravity only), $\text{NE}(q,\dot q,0)=C(q,\dot q)\dot q+G(q)$ (Coriolis+gravity), $\text{NE}(q,0,e_i)=M(q)e_i$ (one column of the mass matrix at a time).

## Following a reference trajectory in joint space

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-03-ex1-naive-tracking-instability" />

Given a smooth reference trajectory $q^{\text{ref}}_{0:T}$, the naive approach (read off $\ddot q^{\text{ref}}_t$ directly, e.g. via a finite-difference of the reference, and command $u=M(q)\ddot q^{\text{ref}}_t+F(q,\dot q)$) is unstable: tiny acceleration errors accumulate over time with nothing pulling the system back toward the reference. The fix is exactly DYN-02's PD idea, layered on top:

$$\ddot q_t^* = \ddot q_t^{\text{ref}} + K_p(q_t^{\text{ref}}-q_t) + K_d(\dot q_t^{\text{ref}}-\dot q_t)$$

then apply inverse dynamics, $u=M(q)\ddot q_t^*+F(q,\dot q)$. This is a genuinely elegant result: **every joint now behaves exactly like DYN-02's 1D point mass around the reference trajectory**, with the same $(K_p,K_d)$ damping-regime classification applying directly, since the nonlinear $M(q),F(q,\dot q)$ terms have been *exactly cancelled* by inverse dynamics, leaving pure point-mass-shaped error dynamics behind.

## Operational space control: the dynamic analogue of IK

Recall KIN-03's inverse kinematics: $q^*=\operatorname*{argmin}_q\|\phi(q)-y^*\|_C^2+\|q-q_0\|_W^2$. **Operational space control** asks the exact same question one derivative level up, for accelerations and torques instead of positions:

$$u^* = \operatorname*{argmin}_u\ \|\ddot\phi(q)-\ddot y^*\|_C^2+\|u\|_H^2$$

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-03-ex3-operational-space-derivation" />

Working through the linearization (product rule on $\dot\phi=J\dot q$, giving $\ddot\phi=J\ddot q+\dot J\dot q$, then substituting forward dynamics $\ddot q=M^{-1}(u-F)$) gives, with $T:=JM^{-1}$:

$$u^* = T^\sharp(\ddot y^*-\dot J\dot q+TF), \qquad T^\sharp = (T^\top CT+H)^{-1}T^\top C$$

exactly KIN-03/[MATH-02B](/course/MATH/MATH-02B)'s Gauss-Newton closed form again, now with $T=JM^{-1}$ playing the role of the Jacobian and $(\ddot y^*-\dot J\dot q+TF)$ playing the role of the target residual. The desired task-space acceleration $\ddot y_t^*$ is itself generated the same DYN-02 way: $\ddot y_t^* = \ddot y_t^{\text{ref}}+K_p(y_t^{\text{ref}}-y_t)+K_d(\dot y_t^{\text{ref}}-\dot y_t)$.

Operational space control literally makes the end-effector *feel* like DYN-02's 1D point mass, directly, even though the actual actuators are joint torques several linkage-levels away, exactly what you want when the task (e.g. "reach this point," "track this end-effector trajectory") is naturally specified in task space rather than joint space.

## Multiple tasks

Both the IK and operational-space formulations generalize immediately to a "big" kinematic map $\Phi(q)$ stacking several feature maps (position, orientation, multiple end-effectors, obstacle-avoidance margins, ...): $q^*=\operatorname*{argmin}_q\|q-q_0\|_W^2+\|\Phi(q)\|^2$ (statics) or $u^*=\operatorname*{argmin}_u\|u\|_H^2+\|\Phi(q)\|^2$ (dynamics), the same regularized-least-squares machinery handling arbitrarily many simultaneous objectives at once, simply by stacking rows of $\Phi$.

## Summary

- $M(q)\ddot q+F(q,\dot q)=u$ gives inverse dynamics ($u$ from desired $\ddot q$) and forward dynamics ($\ddot q$ from applied $u$, used for simulation); the Newton-Euler recursion computes inverse dynamics in $O(n)$ without forming $M(q)$ explicitly.
- Naively tracking a reference trajectory's raw accelerations is unstable; adding DYN-02's PD correction to the desired acceleration, then cancelling the nonlinearity via inverse dynamics, makes every joint behave exactly like a 1D point mass around the reference.
- Operational space control is the acceleration/torque-level analogue of KIN-03's IK, with the identical Gauss-Newton-shaped closed form, $T=JM^{-1}$ standing in for the Jacobian.
- Both the position-level and acceleration-level formulations stack multiple simultaneous task objectives via a single "big" kinematic map $\Phi(q)$.
