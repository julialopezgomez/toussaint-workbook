## Optimal control: problem formulation

**Discrete time**: given a controlled dynamic system $x_{t+1}=f(x_t,u_t)$ (state $x_t$, e.g. $(q_t,\dot q_t)$; control $u_t$), define a cost $J^\pi=\sum_{t=0}^Tc(x_t,u_t)+\phi(x_T)$ under a policy $\pi:(x,t)\mapsto u$; find $\pi^*=\operatorname*{argmin}_\pi J^\pi$.

**Continuous time**: the same idea with $\dot x=f(x,u)$ and $J^\pi=\int_0^Tc(x(t),u(t))\,dt+\phi(x(T))$.

## The Bellman principle and the Bellman equation

> **Bellman's principle.** An optimal policy has the property that, whatever the initial state and initial decision are, the *remaining* decisions must themselves constitute an optimal policy with regard to the state resulting from that first decision.

Define the **value function** (optimal cost-to-go) $V_t(x)=\min_\pi\left[\sum_{s=t}^Tc(x_s,u_s)+\phi(x_T)\right]_{x_t=x}$. Bellman's principle turns this into a recursive equation:

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-04-ex1-bellman-derivation" />

$$\boxed{V_t(x) = \min_u\big[c(x,u)+V_{t+1}(f(x,u))\big]}$$

the **Bellman equation**, solved backward in time from $V_T(x)=\phi(x)$ (dynamic programming). The $\operatorname*{argmin}$ at each state gives the optimal control, $\pi_t^*(x)=\operatorname*{argmin}_u[\,\cdots]$.

## Continuous time: the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation

The exact same idea, continuous version: $V(x,t)=\min_\pi\left[\int_t^Tc(x(s),u(s))\,ds+\phi(x(T))\right]_{x(t)=x}$. Applying the discrete Bellman equation over an infinitesimal step $dt$ and Taylor-expanding $V$ to first order gives:

$$-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(x,t) = \min_u\Big[c(x,u)+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}f(x,u)\Big] \qquad\textbf{(Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman, HJB)}$$

(Applying the discrete Bellman equation at $V_t$ and $V_{t+dt}$, expanding $V(x(t+dt),t+dt)\approx V(x,t)+\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}dt+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\dot x\,dt$, and cancelling $V(x,t)$ from both sides, leaves exactly this equation in the $dt\to0$ limit, mirroring MATH-00's Taylor-expansion-based derivations throughout this whole workbook.)

For an **infinite horizon** ($J^\pi=\int_0^\infty c\,dt$, time-invariant cost), the optimal value function and policy become **stationary** ($V(x,t)=V(x)$, no explicit $t$-dependence), and the HJB/Bellman equations simplify to $0=\min_u[c(x,u)+\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}f(x,u)]$ / $V(x)=\min_u[c(x,u)+V(f(x,u))]$.

## Linear-Quadratic control and the Riccati equation

The HJB equation is a hard PDE in general, but becomes tractable for **linear dynamics** $\dot x=Ax+Bu$ and **quadratic costs** $c(x,u)=x^\top Qx+u^\top Ru$, $\phi(x_T)=x_T^\top Fx_T$ (constant/linear cost terms are dropped: constants are irrelevant to the argmin, linear terms can be absorbed by redefining $x$ or $u$). In this **LQ case**, the value function is *always* exactly quadratic, $V(x,t)=x^\top P(t)x$:

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-04-ex2-riccati-derivation" />

$$\boxed{-\dot P = A^\top P+PA-PBR^{-1}B^\top P+Q} \qquad\textbf{(Riccati differential equation)}, \qquad u^* = -R^{-1}B^\top Px$$

solved backward from $P(T)=F$. The optimal controller $u^*=-Kx$ with $K=R^{-1}B^\top P$ is the **Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR)**; for the infinite-horizon case, $\dot P=0$ gives the (usually numerically-solved) **Algebraic Riccati Equation (ARE)**: $0=A^\top P+PA-PBR^{-1}B^\top P+Q$.

## LQR *is* PD control

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-04-ex3-lqr-point-mass" />

For state $x=(q-r,\dot q-\dot r)$ (tracking a reference $r(t)$), the LQR gain matrix splits naturally as $K=[K_p\ K_d]$, giving $u^*=K_p(r-q)+K_d(\dot r-\dot q)$: **LQR is exactly PD control**, with the gains derived from first principles (via the cost weights $Q,R$) rather than hand-tuned. This is a genuinely satisfying closure: DYN-02 introduced PD control as a reasonable heuristic; here it falls out as the mathematically optimal controller for a natural quadratic-cost objective.

## The grand unification: one structure, four names

<ExerciseCard id="DYN-04-ex4-unification-conceptual" />

$$
\begin{aligned}
\textbf{Optimal control:}&\quad \min_\pi\ J^\pi=\int_0^Tc(x(t),u(t))\,dt+\phi(x(T))\\
\textbf{Inverse kinematics (KIN-03):}&\quad \min_q\ \|q-q_0\|_W^2+\|\phi(q)-y^*\|_C^2\\
\textbf{Operational space control (DYN-03):}&\quad \min_u\ \|u\|_H^2+\|\ddot\phi(q)-\ddot y^*\|_C^2\\
\textbf{Trajectory optimization:}&\quad \min_{q_{0:T}}\ \sum_t\|\Psi_t(q_{t-k:t})\|^2+\sum_t\|\Phi_t(q_t)\|^2
\end{aligned}
$$

All four are "minimize a sum of squared control/deviation costs plus squared task costs," at different levels of a hierarchy (single configuration, single acceleration/torque, whole trajectory); all four are solvable by the same Gauss-Newton/SQP machinery from [OPT-04](/course/OPT/OPT-04).

## Trajectory optimization: $k$-order cost terms

Generalizing DYN-01's discretized-path idea properly: a trajectory $q_{0:T}$, cost $f(q_{0:T})=\sum_t\|q_{t-1}-q_t\|_W^2+\sum_t\|\Phi_t(q_t)\|^2$ (control costs plus task costs). More generally, a **$k$-order cost term** $f_t(x_{t-k:t})$ penalizes a $k$-th order finite difference of the trajectory:

| $k$ | $f_t(x_{t-k:t})$ | Penalizes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $x_t-x_{t-1}$ | velocity |
| 2 | $x_t-2x_{t-1}+x_{t-2}$ | acceleration |
| 3 | $x_t-3x_{t-1}+3x_{t-2}-x_{t-3}$ | jerk |

(or the same finite differences applied to a task-space feature $\phi_t$ instead of $x_t$ directly, e.g. penalizing task-space *acceleration*, exactly DYN-03's $\ddot\phi$). Terms of any order stack arbitrarily, and constraints $g_t(x_{t-k:t})\le0,h_t(x_{t-k:t})=0$ can be included directly: $\min_{x_{0:T}}\sum_tf_t(x_{t-k:t})^\top f_t(x_{t-k:t})$ s.t. $\forall t:g_t\le0,h_t=0$, again exactly the Gauss-Newton-shaped NLP from OPT-04, now over an entire trajectory at once rather than one step.

## Summary

- Bellman's principle turns optimal control into a backward recursion (the Bellman/HJB equations), solving for the value function (optimal cost-to-go) and reading off the optimal policy at each state.
- Linear dynamics + quadratic costs make the value function exactly quadratic, giving the Riccati (differential/algebraic) equation and the LQR controller $u^*=-Kx$, $K=R^{-1}B^\top P$.
- For the 1D point mass, LQR's gain matrix splits into exactly $K_p,K_d$: LQR *is* optimal PD control, verified numerically.
- Optimal control, IK, operational space control, and trajectory optimization are the same "control cost + task cost" structure at different levels of a hierarchy, all solvable by the same Gauss-Newton/SQP machinery.
- Trajectory optimization generalizes this to a whole path, with $k$-order finite-difference cost terms penalizing velocity/acceleration/jerk (in configuration or task space) and constraints included directly.
