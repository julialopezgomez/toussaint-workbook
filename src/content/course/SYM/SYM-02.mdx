## Why this matters

A direct note from the source material worth internalizing: what makes first-order (relational) representations valuable isn't really "logical reasoning" as an inference technique, it's the idea of representing knowledge in terms of **objects, properties, and relations**, which generalizes across object identities in a way propositional logic structurally cannot. This is precisely what makes FOL the right foundation for STRIPS/PDDL (SYM-03) and, ultimately, for relational probabilistic models (SYM-04) that unify this generalization power with genuine uncertainty. The specific inference algorithms below matter mainly because **unification** (computing substitutions) is a building block every one of those later formalisms needs.

## Why propositional logic isn't enough

Propositional logic can't express "pits cause breezes in adjacent squares" except by writing one sentence *per square*: it has no notion of objects or general relations between them. **First-order logic (FOL)** fixes this, assuming the world contains **objects** (people, numbers, ...), **relations** (red, brother-of, bigger-than, ...), and **functions** (father-of, one-more-than, ...).

## Syntax, semantics, and quantifiers

FOL adds to propositional logic's connectives: **constants** (KingJohn, 2), **predicates** (Brother, $>$), **variables** ($x,y$), **functions** (Sqrt, FatherOf), and **quantifiers** ($\forall,\exists$). An atomic sentence is $\text{predicate}(\text{term}_1,\ldots)$ or $\text{term}_1=\text{term}_2$; a term is a constant, variable, or function applied to terms. **Universal quantification** $\forall x:P$ is true iff $P$ holds for every object in the model ("Everyone at Berkeley is smart": $\forall x:\text{At}(x,\text{Berkeley})\Rightarrow\text{Smart}(x)$); **existential** $\exists x:P$ iff $P$ holds for *some* object ("Someone at Stanford is smart": $\exists x:\text{At}(x,\text{Stanford})\wedge\text{Smart}(x)$).

**Quantifiers do not commute across type**: $\exists x:\forall y:\text{Loves}(x,y)$ ("someone loves everyone") is a *much* stronger claim than $\forall y:\exists x:\text{Loves}(x,y)$ ("everyone is loved by someone, possibly different people"), even though same-type quantifiers ($\forall x\forall y\equiv\forall y\forall x$, similarly for $\exists$) do commute freely. A **model** for FOL contains objects and relations among them; an **interpretation** maps constant symbols to objects, predicate symbols to relations, function symbols to functional relations.

## Instantiation: from quantified to ground sentences

<ExerciseCard id="SYM-02-ex1-universal-instantiation" />

**Universal instantiation (UI)**: from $\forall v:\alpha$, add $\text{Subst}(\{v/g\},\alpha)$ for *any* ground term $g$ (can be applied repeatedly, to every relevant constant). **Existential instantiation (EI)**: from $\exists v:\alpha$, add $\text{Subst}(\{v/k\},\alpha)$ for a single *fresh* constant $k$ (a **Skolem constant**, never appearing elsewhere), applied only once. Instantiating every quantified sentence this way **propositionalizes** the KB, reducing FOL inference to SYM-01's propositional machinery, in principle: Herbrand's theorem (1930) guarantees that if $\alpha$ is entailed, it's entailed by some finite propositionalized subset, though the search for that subset can loop forever if $\alpha$ is *not* entailed (entailment in FOL is only **semidecidable**, Turing/Church, 1936). Naive propositionalization also generates enormous numbers of irrelevant ground sentences, making it impractical directly, which motivates a smarter approach: **unification**.

## Unification: the matching problem

Instead of instantiating everything, compute *specific* substitutions that make a rule's premises match known facts. $\theta$ **unifies** $\alpha,\beta$ iff $\alpha\theta=\beta\theta$ (applying $\theta$ to both makes them identical).

<ExerciseCard id="SYM-02-ex2-unification-derivation" />

Unification is exactly what lets a single general rule apply to many different concrete situations without explicitly enumerating them, and it's genuinely expensive in general: matching a rule's *conjunctive* premises against a database of facts is an NP-hard constraint-satisfaction problem (a rule with enough $\text{Diff}(\cdot,\cdot)\Rightarrow\ldots$ premises can literally encode graph coloring).

## Generalized Modus Ponens (GMP)

SYM-01's Modus Ponens generalizes directly, using unification to match premises instead of requiring them to already be identical:

$$\frac{p_1',\ p_2',\ \ldots,\ p_n',\qquad(p_1\wedge p_2\wedge\cdots\wedge p_n\Rightarrow q)}{q\theta}\qquad\text{for a substitution }\theta\text{ such that }\forall i:\theta\text{ unifies}(p_i',p_i)$$

<ExerciseCard id="SYM-02-ex3-gmp-derivation" />

(GMP assumes a KB of **definite clauses**, exactly one positive literal, with all variables implicitly universally quantified.) Applying GMP repeatedly, always firing a rule whose instantiated premises are now known, is **forward chaining** for FOL, sound and complete for first-order definite clauses; running it backward from a query is **backward chaining**. **Datalog** (definite clauses with no function symbols) guarantees forward chaining terminates in polynomially many iterations; with function symbols, termination is not guaranteed if the query isn't actually entailed, the same semidecidability limit as before.

## Resolution for FOL

Exactly as in SYM-01, but sentences must first be converted to CNF via a longer procedure (eliminate $\Leftrightarrow,\Rightarrow$; move $\neg$ inward via De Morgan; standardize variable names apart; **Skolemize** existential variables, now replaced by a *function* of the enclosing universal variables rather than a plain constant; drop universal quantifiers; distribute $\wedge$ over $\vee$). The resolution rule itself is the same as SYM-01's, now applied with unification (rather than exact literal matching) to find complementary literals: sound and complete for all of FOL.

## Summary

- **FOL** adds objects, relations, functions, and quantifiers ($\forall,\exists$) to propositional logic; quantifiers of the *same* type commute, of *different* types they generally don't.
- **Universal/existential instantiation** ground quantified sentences into propositional ones; Herbrand's theorem guarantees a finite entailing subset exists, but the search can loop if $\alpha$ isn't entailed (semidecidability).
- **Unification** computes a substitution $\theta$ making two sentences identical, avoiding naive full propositionalization; it's NP-hard in general (a CSP).
- **Generalized Modus Ponens** uses unification to apply a general rule to specific facts; repeated application gives forward/backward chaining, sound and complete for definite clauses (guaranteed-terminating for function-free Datalog).
- **Resolution**, after a longer CNF-conversion procedure (including Skolemization by function, not just constant), remains sound and complete for all of FOL.
