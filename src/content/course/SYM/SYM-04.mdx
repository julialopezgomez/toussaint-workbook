## Why this matters

## Probabilistic Relational Models (PRMs)

Think of a relational database's tables as random variables that depend on each other: a paper's **A**cceptance might depend on its **Q**uality and a reviewer's **M**ood, via a conditional probability table $P(A\mid Q,M)$ that (crucially) doesn't depend on *which specific* paper or reviewer, only on the *values* of $Q,M$, exactly the object-identity-independent generalization SYM-02 flagged as FOL's real value. For a specific domain $D=\{A_1,A_2,P_1,P_2,P_3,R_1,R_2,R_3\}$ (specific papers/reviewers), the PRM defines a probability distribution over all grounded attribute instantiations; learning a PRM means learning these conditional probability tables from data; inference means constructing the full grounded Bayesian network (one random variable per grounded predicate) and running standard graphical-model inference on it.

## Markov Logic Networks (MLNs)

A cleaner, more general formulation: an **MLN** is a set of pairs $\{(F_i,w_i)\}$, a first-order formula $F_i$ and a real-valued weight $w_i$. For a domain $D$, this generates a **factor graph**: one random variable per grounded predicate, one factor per *grounding* of each formula, with

$$F(X;D) \propto \exp\Big\{\sum_i\sum_{\text{true groundings of }F_i\text{ in }X}w_i\Big\}$$

<ExerciseCard id="SYM-04-ex1-mln-grounding-numeric" />

**Worked example** (Friends & Smokers): $w_1=1.5:\ \text{cancer}(x)\leftarrow\text{smoking}(x)$; $w_2=1.1:\ \text{smoking}(x)\leftarrow\text{friends}(x,y)\wedge\text{smoking}(y)$. For domain $D=\{\text{Anna},\text{Bob}\}$, this defines a factor graph over the grounded predicates $\{\text{cancer}(A),\text{cancer}(B),\text{smoking}(A),\text{smoking}(B),\text{friends}(A,A),\text{friends}(A,B),\text{friends}(B,A),\text{friends}(B,B)\}$, with a factor for every satisfied grounding of $F_1,F_2$ across that domain.

<ExerciseCard id="SYM-04-ex2-mln-factor-value-numeric" />

**Inference**: construct the grounded factor graph, then run standard probabilistic inference on it. **Learning**: gradient descent on the (log-)likelihood of the weights $w_i$, often genuinely hard even with complete data (the gradient itself requires inference).

## MLNs' generality

<ExerciseCard id="SYM-04-ex3-infinite-weight-conceptual" />

Two limits are worth knowing, since they locate MLNs precisely relative to everything else in this workbook: taking every predicate to be zero-arity (no relational structure at all) recovers a long list of standard, purely-propositional models as special cases: Markov networks/random fields, Bayesian networks, log-linear/exponential/max-entropy models, Gibbs distributions, Boltzmann machines, logistic regression (ML-02), HMMs, and CRFs (ML-02's brief mention, now precisely located as an MLN special case). Taking every weight $w_i\to\infty$ instead recovers **classical, hard first-order logic** (SYM-02): a formula with infinite weight becomes a hard constraint (any world violating it has zero probability), exactly a classical FOL entailment relationship, no uncertainty at all. **MLNs interpolate continuously between pure logic (infinite weights) and every standard flavor of graphical model (zero-arity predicates)**, which is precisely why they're described as unifying "the logical underpinnings of old AI with the power of statistics."

## Closing the SYM block: uncertainty, structure, and generalization

The theme running through this entire block, worth stating explicitly now that it's been built up piece by piece: **structured (relational/first-order) representations give strong generalization across object identity** (SYM-02); **deterministic rules over that structure are unlearnable from limited data** (SYM-03); **adding calibrated uncertainty on top of the structure is what actually makes learning tractable**, compressing noisy exceptions rather than requiring an ever-more-specific rule for each one (SYM-03, and MLNs' weighted-formula framework generally). Probabilistic relational modeling isn't a magic fix for every AI problem, but it is, as of today, close to the only principled way to represent and learn genuinely uncertain, genuinely generalizing knowledge about a world of objects, properties, and relations, exactly the setting TAMP operates in.

## Summary

- **PRMs** treat a relational database's attributes as random variables with conditional probability tables independent of object identity; a specific domain grounds them into a full Bayesian network.
- **MLNs** are a cleaner formalization: weighted FOL formulas $\{(F_i,w_i)\}$ define, for any domain $D$, a grounded factor graph $F(X;D)\propto\exp\{\sum_i\sum_{\text{true groundings}}w_i\}$.
- **Zero-arity predicates** collapse MLNs to standard graphical models (Markov/Bayesian networks, logistic regression, CRFs, ...); **infinite weights** recover classical hard FOL. MLNs interpolate continuously between the two.
- The SYM block's throughline: relational structure gives generalization; deterministic rules over that structure are unlearnable; calibrated uncertainty is what makes learning tractable, exactly the setting TAMP needs.
