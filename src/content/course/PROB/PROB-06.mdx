## The Laplace approximation

The source motivates this with a scenario directly from robotics: suppose you want $q(x)=p(x\mid D)\propto p(D\mid x)p(x)$, where $x$ is a stochastically-controlled robot path and $p(x)$ a nice Gaussian prior over controls, but $p(D\mid x)$ (e.g. a collision-avoidance likelihood) is computed by a black-box collision-detection package: it gives you values and gradients, but is nowhere near an analytic, closed-form density. $q(x)$ simply cannot be expressed analytically, so it needs to be *approximated*.

> **Definition 5.10 (Laplace approximation).** Given a smooth distribution $q(x)$, define its Laplace approximation as $\tilde q(x)=\exp\{-\tilde E(x)\}$, where $\tilde E(x) = E(x^*)+\tfrac12(x-x^*)^\top\nabla^2E(x^*)(x-x^*)$ is the 2nd-order Taylor expansion of the energy $E(x)=-\log q(x)$ (PROB-04's energy view) at the mode $x^*=\operatorname*{argmin}_xE(x)=\operatorname*{argmax}_xq(x)$.

The linear term vanishes because you Taylor-expand specifically *at the mode*, where $\nabla E(x^*)=0$; since the energy's quadratic approximation is a parabola, its exponential $\tilde q(x)$ is automatically Gaussian. Computing $x^*$ is itself an ordinary optimization problem, often solved via Newton's method ([OPT-01](/course/OPT/OPT-01)), which conveniently computes the Hessian of $E$ at every step anyway, so at the optimum you already have exactly the precision matrix the Laplace approximation needs, for free.

<ExerciseCard id="PROB-06-ex2-laplace-derivation" />

The result, $p(x) \approx N(x\mid x^*, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2 = \left(-\dfrac{d^2\log p}{dx^2}\Big|_{x^*}\right)^{-1}$, is exactly Definition 5.10 in 1D: literally MATH-00's second-order Taylor expansion, applied to $\log p$ rather than $p$, then exponentiated and matched to a Gaussian's exponent form. The multivariable version replaces the second derivative with (negative) the Hessian of $\log p$ at the mode: $\Sigma = \left(-H_{\log p}(x^*)\right)^{-1}$.

## Worked example

For $\log \tilde p(x) = -\tfrac14x^4+\tfrac12x^2$ (an unnormalized, actually bimodal density with modes at $x=\pm1$), at the mode $x^*=1$: $\dfrac{d^2}{dx^2}\log\tilde p\Big|_{x=1} = -3(1)^2+1 = -2$, so $\sigma^2 = (-(-2))^{-1}=0.5$. The Laplace approximation there is $N(x\mid 1, 0.5)$. *(Verified with SymPy while authoring this module.)* Note this is only a *local* approximation around the mode at $x=1$, it says nothing about the second mode at $x=-1$, an important limitation: Laplace approximation assumes (or picks) a single dominant mode.

## Variational inference and Fisher information

**Variational inference** generalizes the same underlying idea (approximate a hard-to-work-with distribution with an easy one) beyond just a local Taylor expansion at a mode:

> **Definition 5.11 (variational inference).** Given a distribution $p(x)$ and a parameterized family $q(x\mid\beta)$, the variational approximation of $p(x)$ is $\operatorname*{argmin}_qD(q\|p)$.

I.e., instead of matching a Taylor expansion locally at the mode, variational inference poses the approximation as an explicit optimization problem: choose the member of the family $q(x\mid\beta)$ (e.g. a Gaussian family, varying over $\beta=(\mu,\Sigma)$) that minimizes the KL divergence $D(q\|p)$ (PROB-03) to the true, hard-to-work-with $p$, over the *whole* distribution, not just locally around one point.

**Fisher information** measures how sharply peaked a distribution's log-likelihood is around its mode, formally, it's (the negative expectation of) the same second-derivative-of-log-probability quantity that appears in the Laplace approximation's variance formula, connecting "how much information a sample carries about a parameter" directly to "how tightly the Laplace-approximated posterior would be concentrated." (Full disclosure: the source's own treatment of the **Fisher information metric**, §5.6, is explicitly left as a "TODO" in Toussaint's notes, framed only as "the natural metric for comparing two probability distributions $p(x;\beta_1)$ and $p(x;\beta_2)$," directly generalizing OPT-01/MATH-05's steepest-descent-under-a-metric idea to the space of probability distributions themselves, i.e. the "natural gradient." This module covers it only at that same conceptual level the source leaves it at, rather than inventing a fuller derivation the source itself doesn't provide.)

## Summary

- Laplace approximation: $p(x)\approx N(x\mid x^*,\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2=(-\log p''(x^*))^{-1}$, a direct application of MATH-00's second-order Taylor expansion to $\log p$.
- It's a *local* approximation around a single chosen mode; multimodal distributions need care (which mode, or a mixture of local approximations).
- Variational inference generalizes the same idea to a global (not just local) optimization over an approximating family; Fisher information formalizes "how peaked is the log-likelihood" and appears directly in the Laplace variance formula.
