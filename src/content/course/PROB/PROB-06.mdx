## The Laplace approximation

<ExerciseCard id="PROB-06-ex2-laplace-derivation" />

The result, $p(x) \approx N(x\mid x^*, \sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2 = \left(-\dfrac{d^2\log p}{dx^2}\Big|_{x^*}\right)^{-1}$, is the **Laplace approximation**: literally MATH-00's second-order Taylor expansion, applied to $\log p$ rather than $p$, then exponentiated and matched to a Gaussian's exponent form. The multivariable version replaces the second derivative with (negative) the Hessian of $\log p$ at the mode: $\Sigma = \left(-H_{\log p}(x^*)\right)^{-1}$.

## Worked example

For $\log \tilde p(x) = -\tfrac14x^4+\tfrac12x^2$ (an unnormalized, actually bimodal density with modes at $x=\pm1$), at the mode $x^*=1$: $\dfrac{d^2}{dx^2}\log\tilde p\Big|_{x=1} = -3(1)^2+1 = -2$, so $\sigma^2 = (-(-2))^{-1}=0.5$. The Laplace approximation there is $N(x\mid 1, 0.5)$. *(Verified with SymPy while authoring this module.)* Note this is only a *local* approximation around the mode at $x=1$, it says nothing about the second mode at $x=-1$, an important limitation: Laplace approximation assumes (or picks) a single dominant mode.

## Variational inference and Fisher information, briefly

**Variational inference** generalizes the same underlying idea (approximate a hard-to-work-with distribution with an easy one) beyond just a local Taylor expansion at a mode: it poses the approximation as an explicit optimization problem, choosing the easy distribution (e.g. from a Gaussian family) that minimizes $D(q\|p)$ (the KL divergence from PROB-03) over the whole distribution, not just locally.

**Fisher information** measures how sharply peaked a distribution's log-likelihood is around its mode, formally, it's (the negative expectation of) the same second-derivative-of-log-probability quantity that appears in the Laplace approximation's variance formula, connecting "how much information a sample carries about a parameter" directly to "how tightly the Laplace-approximated posterior would be concentrated."

## Summary

- Laplace approximation: $p(x)\approx N(x\mid x^*,\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma^2=(-\log p''(x^*))^{-1}$, a direct application of MATH-00's second-order Taylor expansion to $\log p$.
- It's a *local* approximation around a single chosen mode; multimodal distributions need care (which mode, or a mixture of local approximations).
- Variational inference generalizes the same idea to a global (not just local) optimization over an approximating family; Fisher information formalizes "how peaked is the log-likelihood" and appears directly in the Laplace variance formula.
