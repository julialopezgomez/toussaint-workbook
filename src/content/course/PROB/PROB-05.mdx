## Why this matters

Gaussians are the workhorse distribution of robotics and ML: Kalman filters, Gaussian processes, variational inference, and the Laplace approximation (next module) all lean on the specific algebraic identities this module covers. Because these identities get used constantly but are easy to misremember under pressure, this module is deliberately reference-style, expect to come back to it.

## The product of two Gaussians

<ExerciseCard id="PROB-05-ex2-product-derivation" />

The full result (mean and variance both) for 1D Gaussians $N(x\mid a,A)$, $N(x\mid b,B)$:

$$\sigma_c^2 = \left(\frac1A+\frac1B\right)^{-1}, \qquad \mu_c = \sigma_c^2\left(\frac{a}{A}+\frac{b}{B}\right)$$

This is a **precision-weighted average**: the combined mean leans toward whichever input has the tighter (smaller) variance, i.e. more confidence. The multivariable generalization replaces $1/A$ with $A^{-1}$ throughout (matrix inverse instead of scalar reciprocal); the reference identities for that general case are collected below.

## Worked example

$N(x\mid 2,1)$ and $N(x\mid 4,2)$: $\sigma_c^2 = (1/1+1/2)^{-1} = 0.6\overline{6}$, $\mu_c = 0.6\overline{6}(2/1+4/2) = 0.6\overline{6}(4) \approx 2.667$. *(Verified with NumPy while authoring this module.)* The combined mean sits closer to $2$ than to $4$, since the first Gaussian ($A=1$) is more confident (tighter) than the second ($B=2$).

## Reference: further Gaussian identities

The source note collects a larger set of identities than this module derives in full; these are worth knowing exist so you can look them up precisely when needed, rather than re-deriving them each time:

- **Marginal & conditional** of a jointly-Gaussian $(x,y)$: both are Gaussian, with the conditional's mean shifted by a term proportional to the cross-covariance (this is the exact mechanism behind the Kalman filter's update step).
- **Convolution**: $\int_x N(x\mid a,A)\,N(y-x\mid b,B)\,dx = N(y\mid a+b, A+B)$, variances add under convolution.
- **Expectations**: $\mathbb{E}_x\{x\}=a$, $\mathbb{E}_x\{xx^\top\}=A+aa^\top$.
- **Entropy**: $H(N(a,A)) = \tfrac12\log|2\pi e A|$.
- **KL divergence between two Gaussians**: a closed form in terms of $a,A,b,B$ (no sampling needed).

## Summary

- Gaussians are closed under products, marginals, and conditionals, the property that makes sequential Bayesian filtering with fixed-size representations possible.
- Product of two Gaussians: precisions add ($1/\sigma_c^2 = 1/A+1/B$); the combined mean is a precision-weighted average.
- A fuller reference set of identities (marginal/conditional, convolution, expectations, entropy, KL) exists in the source note for lookup as needed.
