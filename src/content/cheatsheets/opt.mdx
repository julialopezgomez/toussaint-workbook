## Definitions

- **Newton's method** (OPT-01): use the local quadratic (2nd-order Taylor) model of $f$ to jump straight to its minimum; needs the Hessian, converges fast near a minimum but can diverge or head uphill far from one, hence line search.
- **Gauss-Newton / quasi-Newton** (OPT-02): for least-squares objectives $f=\phi^\top\phi$, approximate the Hessian as $J^\top J$ (cheap, always PSD); BFGS instead *builds up* a Hessian approximation from observed gradient differences, no second derivatives needed at all.
- **KKT conditions** (OPT-03): the four conditions (stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementary slackness) that characterize a constrained local optimum, generalizing "$\nabla f=0$" to the constrained case.
- **Convexity / interior-point methods** (OPT-04): a convex $f$ has no bad local minima (every local min is global); log-barrier/augmented-Lagrangian methods handle inequality constraints by making the objective blow up near the infeasible boundary, turning a constrained problem into a sequence of unconstrained ones.
- **Stochastic optimization** (OPT-05): use a cheap, noisy gradient estimate (one data point/mini-batch) instead of the full gradient; convergence needs a *decreasing* step size schedule to average out the noise.
- **Bayesian optimization / bandits** (OPT-06): model the unknown objective with a Gaussian Process (or a bandit's per-arm estimate), then use an acquisition function to decide where to query next, balancing exploring uncertain regions against exploiting known-good ones.

| Gauss-Newton Hessian approx. | $\nabla^2f(x)\approx2J^\top J$ | OPT-02 |
| BFGS secant equation | $\hat H\delta=y$ | OPT-02 |
| Lagrangian | $L(x,\lambda,\kappa)=f(x)+\lambda^\top g(x)+\kappa^\top h(x)$ | OPT-03 |
| KKT stationarity | $\nabla f(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla g_i(x)+\sum_j\kappa_j\nabla h_j(x)=0$ | OPT-03 |
| Complementary slackness | $\lambda_ig_i(x)=0$ for all $i$ | OPT-03 |
| Log-barrier objective | $B(x,\mu)=f(x)-\mu\sum_i\log(-g_i(x))$ | OPT-04 |

## Assumptions & applicability

- Newton's method needs $\nabla^2 f(x)$ to be positive definite to guarantee a *descent* direction; if it isn't (e.g. near a saddle), the raw Newton step can go uphill — line search / Levenberg-Marquardt damping ($\lambda$) is what keeps it safe.
- Gauss-Newton's $J^\top J\approx\nabla^2f$ approximation is only good when the residuals $\phi_i(x)$ are individually small or the problem is close to linear; it drops the $\sum_i\phi_i(x)\nabla^2\phi_i(x)$ term entirely.
- KKT conditions are only *sufficient* for optimality (not just necessary) when the problem is convex; for non-convex problems they characterize a stationary point, not necessarily a global (or even local) minimum.
- SGD's convergence bound requires a **decreasing** step-size schedule ($\sum\alpha_k\to\infty$, $\sum\alpha_k^2<\infty$); a constant step size converges to a noise floor, not to $\nabla f=0$.
- GP-based Bayesian optimization scales poorly with data size (the $(K+\sigma_0^2I)^{-1}$ inversion is $O(n^3)$) — fine for the few-hundred-query regime BayesOpt is usually used for, not for large datasets.

## Common mistakes

- Using plain Newton's method without a line search or damping far from the optimum — can diverge or oscillate.
- Forgetting the $\ge0$ sign constraint on $\lambda_i$ (dual feasibility) — only inequality-constraint multipliers are sign-constrained, equality-constraint multipliers $\kappa_j$ are free.
- Confusing the log-barrier's $\mu\to0$ limit (approaches the true constrained problem) with $\mu\to\infty$ — a larger $\mu$ makes the barrier *stronger*, pushing the solution further from the boundary, the opposite of what you want asymptotically.
- Treating BayesOpt's acquisition function as the objective itself — $\alpha(x;D)$ says where to *query next*, not what the optimum value is; the actual best point found so far is a separate running quantity.

## Decision rule

- Smooth, unconstrained, cheap-to-evaluate Hessian available? Newton's method (OPT-01). Least-squares-shaped objective, Hessian expensive? Gauss-Newton or BFGS (OPT-02).
- Constrained problem? Check convexity first: convex → interior-point/log-barrier (OPT-04) is reliable; non-convex → SQP or augmented Lagrangian, and don't expect a global-optimality guarantee.
- Can't compute an exact gradient (noisy, stochastic, or black-box objective)? Cheap per-sample gradient available → SGD/Adam-family (OPT-05); no gradient at all → CMA-ES or Bayesian optimization (OPT-06), the latter especially when each evaluation is expensive (real experiments, hyperparameter search).
